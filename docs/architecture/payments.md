# Payment research for the marketplace bridge model

Rechecked **2026-09-15**, using official provider documentation and the RBI's primary text. No provider was contacted, no credentials were used, and no integration was executed. This note updates the **product assumption** in the September 14 research: normal provider settlement is the proposed default pending founder/provider approval; proof does not authorize payout. Public documentation establishes capabilities and published restrictions, not Billboard.me's commercial approval.

## Product model and recommendation

Proposed default: **Sponsor pays the provider → provider allocates platform commission and seller proceeds → both settle on their approved normal schedules.** Seller fulfillment and evidence collection proceed independently. A seller may receive settlement before delivering the campaign or uploading proof. Do not market the transaction as escrow, a guarantee of performance, or proof-dependent release.

**Deferred settlement is optional functionality, not a prerequisite for this model.** Removing an application-controlled hold simplifies the payment lifecycle and removes the need to validate proof-gated release for v1. It does **not** establish a turnover/KYC exemption, eliminate refunds or chargebacks, prevent provider risk holds, or make the platform free of contractual liability. The provider's approved account/charge configuration still matters.

Keep Razorpay Route and Cashfree Easy Split as **conditional candidates**. Re-evaluate Stripe Connect for an India-to-India direct-charge or eligible domestic destination-charge flow: those commission paths are expressly documented. Dodo remains excluded under its current marketplace policy. Select one provider after the external questions below are answered; do not implement multiple providers.

**Recommended product responsibility:** seller supplies the physical advertising and agreed evidence; sponsor supplies artwork/approvals and pays; Billboard.me provides discovery/share pages, agreement workflow, payment integration, records, support and moderation. This is a product boundary, **not a legal conclusion** about liability. The provider/bank/network controls its payment-dispute process; a founder's informal decision cannot override a network chargeback. Actual contracts determine who accepts or contests a refund/dispute and bears an unrecoverable loss.

## India primary-source check

The RBI's **2025 PA Directions** distinguish multiple situations. Section 13 covers merchant due diligence and marketplace seller onboarding. Section 16, Table 1, permitted debits (iv) sets turnover and payer-payee conditions for the described third-party-payment path. That provision does not tie those conditions to a platform holding funds until proof. Section 8 requires payment-dispute mechanisms and stakeholder agreements defining responsibilities; section 18(h) preserves applicable customer chargeback rights. [RBI primary text](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12896&Mode=0).

**Interpretation limit:** these findings do not establish whether Billboard.me is a PA, who must be the provider's merchant in a proposed direct-charge configuration, or whether a particular alternative funds flow qualifies. **Requires external validation:** provider/compliance confirmation and qualified Indian advice for the actual entity, contracts, tax/invoicing and settlement flow. Normal settlement is not a self-certified regulatory exemption. Equally, the Route-specific access finding must not be generalized into a claim that all small sellers or every direct-charge marketplace are prohibited.

## Requires external validation before payment implementation

1. **Eligibility:** Billboard.me's operating entity and turnover; physical sponsorship category; acceptance of Indian individuals; provider access. For Route, explicitly resolve its published threshold. For Cashfree/Stripe, establish the approved flow rather than assuming Route's rules map identically.
2. **Exact funds flow:** sponsor → provider → platform fee and seller share on ordinary schedules; whether direct or destination-style collection is approved; which party is merchant/payee; allowed buyer geography/currency and methods.
3. **Commercial and legal roles:** seller/sponsor terms, invoicing, fee refunds, taxes/withholding, dispute responder, unrecoverable loss liability, advance-booking/service-delivery restrictions. Product labels cannot settle these questions.
4. **Operations:** KYC before collection/settlement as required, provider risk holds, failed/returned settlement, refunds after payout, bank references, fees and reconciliation. Obtain account-specific schedules; do not promise “instant” based on marketing examples.
5. **Sandbox proof:** approved seller setup → payment → fee allocation → normal seller settlement with **no proof event and no application release command**, plus duplicate/out-of-order events, failed settlement, refund and dispute scenarios. Sandbox functionality is not commercial approval.

Optional future work: a provider-approved deferred settlement service, evaluated only if validation shows value. It needs separate explicit terms and capability testing. It should not block the bridge MVP or silently appear as default campaign behavior.

## Documentation and state-model changes recommended

- Replace the old mandatory `payment → hold → proof approved → release → payout` path with **parallel commercial and fulfillment lifecycles**: payment/settlement and campaign/evidence.
- Settlement can be pending/settled/failed/returned or provider-blocked independently of campaign status. A campaign may be unfulfilled with settlement already successful; an executed campaign may have failed settlement.
- Remove proof approval and founder release as prerequisites to normal settlement. Keep provider-imposed blocks distinguishable from any future product hold.
- The success metric remains a conjunction: a completed paid campaign, sponsor-reviewed/acknowledged evidence with provenance, and successful seller settlement. **It does not prescribe the order in which those facts occur.** Count all facts for the same campaign without equating transfer creation with bank receipt.
- Retain separate payment attempt, fee allocation, refund, seller settlement and dispute records; signed/deduplicated webhooks and reconciliation remain P0. Proof remains useful for sponsor confidence and dispute evidence without controlling money release.
- Keep one provider adapter and no wallet/custom escrow. Do not make `releasePayoutAfterProof` a mandatory adapter interface. Any optional hold capability should be explicit and absent from v1's normal command path.

## Implementation constraints retained from the earlier research

Keep payment authorization/capture, fee/share allocation and bank settlement distinct. Do not leave authorization open until an event; Razorpay currently documents a three-day capture window. [Capture settings](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/capture-settings/).

Endpoint idempotency is specific: Razorpay’s documented transfer idempotency header covers Direct Transfers, not every payment-linked transfer/refund endpoint. Use durable local operation keys, unique provider references, signed raw-body webhook validation and duplicate/out-of-order handling. Query unknown outcomes before retrying. [Route idempotency](https://razorpay.com/docs/api/payments/route/direct-transfers-idempotent-request/), [Webhook FAQ](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/faqs/).

Cashfree’s split-submission deadline is different from a vendor settlement delay. Missing a required split can misallocate money to the platform; ordinary allocation must run on time even though evidence is independent. Confirm endpoint/event versions and account-specific schedules during integration. [Split timing](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/settlements/delay/order-level), [Payment webhooks](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/api-reference/payments/latest/payments/webhooks).

## Deployment verification

OpenNext's own support page says **all Next.js 16 minor and patch releases are supported**, which nominally includes 16.3.5. It uses Next's Node runtime on Cloudflare Workers, not a full ordinary Node process, and flags unsupported Node middleware. This is a compatibility claim, **not verification that the current repository builds and runs correctly**. [OpenNext support](https://opennext.js.org/cloudflare).

Cloudflare's current guide, updated August 25, 2026, now recommends **vinext**, an API reimplementation on Vite, and explicitly labels it beta. Its OpenNext adapter documentation remains available. This is a meaningful framework/runtime choice requiring evaluation; it is not a reason to automatically migrate a first-transaction app. [Current Cloudflare Next.js guide](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/framework-guides/web-apps/nextjs/), [OpenNext adapter configuration](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/framework-guides/web-apps/opennext/).

| Concern | Verified fact and implication |
| --- | --- |
| Postgres driver | Cloudflare supports Postgres.js with Hyperdrive (minimum 3.4.5), so the repository's ^3.4.9 is above that minimum; `pg` is currently recommended by Cloudflare but this alone does not justify replacing the existing driver. Validate the chosen driver under actual concurrent requests. [Postgres.js on Hyperdrive](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/examples/connect-to-postgres/postgres-drivers-and-libraries/postgres-js/). |
| Connection lifetime | OpenNext warns against sharing database clients with request-bound I/O across Workers requests. Use the selected platform's recommended request-scoped lifecycle and pooling rather than assuming the existing lazily created, module-cached client is portable. [OpenNext database guide](https://opennext.js.org/cloudflare/howtos/db). |
| Better Auth | Better Auth documents Drizzle/Postgres adapters and ORM schema generation. Pin compatible packages and generate/review auth schema through Drizzle migrations; do not run Better Auth's built-in migration machinery with the Drizzle adapter. No exact-repo Workers auth behavior was tested here. [Drizzle adapter](https://better-auth.com/docs/adapters/drizzle), [Database/migrations](https://better-auth.com/docs/concepts/database). |
| Sharp | Sharp's standard build requires a Node-API-compatible runtime. Its optional WASM package requires multithreading and does not support single-threaded environments; this is not a generic Workers portability promise. Don't base Workers media processing on untested native Sharp. [Sharp installation](https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/install/). |
| Images/storage | OpenNext's documented image optimization uses Cloudflare Images bindings or a custom loader, with compatibility/cost considerations. Canonical SVG templates do not need runtime raster processing. Private proof originals require access controls independently of public transformed marketing images. [OpenNext image optimization](https://opennext.js.org/cloudflare/howtos/image). |

**Recommended deployment gate:** a timeboxed spike proves the exact dependency versions, build, server route, real nonproduction DB connection under repeated/concurrent requests, planned auth session flow, raw-body webhook verification, and operator-triggered/scheduled reconciliation. Validate private upload/read and image processing when MEDIA-001 enables native files; this is not a proof-before-payment launch gate. If Cloudflare adds unresolved adapter/media complexity, select a conventional managed Node deployment for the first transaction and retain Supabase Postgres and portable object storage. This is an engineering recommendation based on integration surface, not a benchmark result. Do not replace Next.js, add Hyperdrive, or introduce another storage provider in this documentation task.
